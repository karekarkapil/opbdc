# Data room checklist

*Companion to Chapter 19, "Building for Value", of* 1 Person, Billion Dollar Conglomerate *(2026 edition). Last reviewed: September 2026.*

## What this is

A **data room** is the folder a buyer or investor examines during due diligence, the process in which they check a company before paying for it or putting money in. This file lists everything a buyer's due diligence is likely to ask for, and gives you the brief for the agent that keeps the folder current every month. When an offer comes, you are ready in a week, not a season.

For a company run by agents, two parts matter more than usual: **the written company** (proof the business runs on a machine, not on your memory) and **proof that it runs without you**.

> This is not legal, tax or financial advice. What a buyer asks for, and what you may share and when, depends on the deal, the country and the industry. Before sharing anything, and especially anything containing personal data or confidential contracts, take advice from a lawyer and an adviser who sells companies like yours. Share under a confidentiality agreement.

## How to use it

1. Create a `data-room/` folder for each company on day one, with the sections below. Each company has its own; never mix companies (Chapter 17).
2. Brief an agent (below) to update it after every monthly close.
3. Read the agent's monthly report in the monthly part of your [operating rhythm](../../playbooks/weekly-review.md). Fix any gap it flags.
4. Once a year, walk the folder as if you were the buyer: what would worry you?

## The brief for the agent that maintains it

> **Goal:** Keep this company's data room complete and current, so that it could be shared with a serious buyer within a week. Run after each monthly close is signed off.
>
> **Context:** Read this checklist, the latest signed monthly close, the CEO dashboard history, the context kit's last-checked dates, the permission matrix, the incident log, the compliance calendar, the decision log (for the path decision) and the moat audits. Company: [name]. Data room folder: [location].
>
> **Constraints:** Read-only access to the source systems; write access only to the data-room folder. Never copy customers' personal data, card or bank details, passwords or keys into the data room; concentration reports use counts and shares only. Never share, send or grant access to the data room to anyone. Never include another company's documents. Do not edit source documents; if one is wrong or missing, report it.
>
> **Done:** Every item in the checklist is present in its latest version, or listed as a gap. The five-numbers history and the concentration reports include last month. A one-page report at the top of the folder lists: what was updated, what is missing, what is out of date (older than its "kept current" interval), and any change that a buyer would notice (a new largest customer, a customer above [20] percent of revenue, a new incident, a contract that cannot transfer).
>
> **Verification:** For each updated item, give the source file and its date. Check three numbers in the five-numbers history against the signed close. List any file you could not open.
>
> **Questions:** Stop and ask before including any document that contains personal data or a confidentiality clause, and whenever a source document contradicts another.

This agent sits at rung 3 of the [trust ladder](../../playbooks/trust-ladder.md) at most: it acts within set limits and reports. Sharing the data room with anyone is an irreversible act and stays yours.
