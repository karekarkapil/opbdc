# Customer conversations

*Companion to Chapter 5, "A Problem Worth Solving", of* 1 Person, Billion Dollar Conglomerate *(2026 edition). Last reviewed: September 2026.*

## What this is

The rule is **ten conversations before any building.** Not surveys, not a landing page with a signup form, not a poll. Ten real conversations with people who have the problem, each at least twenty minutes long. Agents help you prepare and make sense of them. The conversation itself is yours.

This file holds a question guide, an outreach template to personalize, a summary format for the [customer file](../context-kit/customer-file.md), a brief for the agent that writes the summaries, and a tracker for the ten.

## How to use it

1. Have an agent build the list of people to approach, using the list-building brief in [first-ten.md](first-ten.md) ("Brief for the list-building agent"), adapted: people who have the problem, for a conversation, not a sale. The [research agent brief](../briefs/research-agent.md) finds problems, not people.
2. Have an agent draft each outreach message from the template. Edit every one until it sounds like you, and send it yourself.
3. Use the question guide in the call. Take notes, or record with permission.
4. After each call, give your notes to the summary agent. Check the quotes against your notes, then add the summary to the customer file the same day.
5. After ten, score your candidate problems on the [validation scorecard](validation-scorecard.md).

## The question guide

Five principles, then the questions.

- **Ask about the past, not the future.** "Tell me about the last time..." produces facts. "Would you use...?" produces polite lies.
- **Ask what they have tried.** If they have never tried to solve it, it may not hurt enough.
- **Ask what it costs them.** In money, time, lost customers, stress.
- **Do not pitch.** Save your idea for the last five minutes, if at all.
- **Listen for the sentence on the napkin.** In most sets of ten there are one or two sentences that change how you see the problem. Write them down verbatim, with the date.

**Opening (2 minutes)**
- Thank you for the time. I am trying to understand [the area, e.g. how bars manage their drinks stock]. I am not selling anything today.
- Is it all right if I take notes (or record)?

**Their world (5 minutes)**
- Tell me about your role and a typical [day / week / shift].
- Where does [the area] fit into that?

**The last time (10 minutes)**
- Tell me about the last time [the problem] happened. When was it? What happened next?
- What did it cost you? (Money, time, customers, sleep.)
- How often does that happen?

**What they have tried (5 minutes)**
- What do you do about it today?
- What have you tried that did not work? Why did you stop?
- Do you pay for anything, or anyone, to help with this? How much?

**Closing (3 minutes)**
- If you could change one thing about [the area], what would it be?
- Who else should I talk to about this?
- May I come back to you when I have something to show? May I quote you (anonymously)?

**Questions to avoid:** "Would you use...?" "Would you pay for...?" "Don't you think it would be great if...?" "How much would you pay?"

## Outreach template (agent drafts, you edit and send)

> Hi [first name],
>
> I'm [your name]. I [run / am starting] [one line about you, e.g. a small company that makes cocktail mixers for bars in Bengaluru]. I noticed [something specific and real about them: a post, a menu, a review, a mutual contact].
>
> I'm trying to understand how [their kind of business] handles [the problem area], and I'd value 20 minutes of your experience. I'm not selling anything. I'd be glad to share what I learn from the conversations.
>
> Would [two specific times] work, by phone or at [a place near them]?
>
> Thanks,
> [Name]

**Before sending:** read it aloud. Change it until it sounds like you. If it would embarrass you for them to know a machine wrote it, rewrite it. Send from your own address, a few a day.

## Summary format for the customer file

```
## [YYYY-MM-DD] Conversation with [role, business type, size] ([anonymized ID])
**How we met:** [referral / outreach / event]
**Their situation:** [2 to 3 lines]
**The last time it happened:** [what, when, what it cost]
**What they have tried:** [workarounds, tools, people, and what they pay]
**Verbatim quotes:**
- "[exact words]"
- "[exact words]"
**The napkin sentence (if any):** "[exact words]"
**Signals:** severity [1 to 5], frequency [weekly / monthly / rarely], money already spent [yes/no, what]
**Follow-up:** [permission to quote? show them something? referrals?]
```

## Brief for the summary agent

> **Goal:** Turn my notes (or the transcript) of one customer conversation into a summary for the customer file, so the customer's own words are kept and can be found later.
>
> **Context:** Read the customer file's format section and the last three summaries in it. Attached: my notes or the transcript for this call.
>
> **Constraints:** Quote only words that appear in the notes or transcript, exactly. Do not paraphrase inside quotation marks. Do not add interpretation to the quotes section. Remove phone numbers, emails and other personal details.
>
> **Done:** One summary in the format above, ready to paste, plus a separate list of anything I said during the call that sounded like a pitch.
>
> **Verification:** For every quote, give the line or timestamp in the source. Mark any field you could not fill.
>
> **Questions:** Ask me if the notes are ambiguous about who said something, or if a quote seems to contradict the rest of the conversation.
