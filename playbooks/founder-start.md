# Founder start

*Companion to Chapter 1, "The One-Person Conglomerate", of* 1 Person, Billion Dollar Conglomerate *(2026 edition). Last reviewed: September 2026.*

## What this is

The four actions at the end of Chapter 1, on one page, so the machine starts before you build anything: your conglomerate's **mission statement**, a **full-stack self-assessment** in five areas, a **sketch of your first machine** as an organization chart, and the start of your **decision log**. It takes an evening. It is also week 1 of the ninety-day plan in Chapter 21.

## How to use it

1. Copy this file into your own company folder and fill it in, by hand, before you brief a single agent. The point is your own thinking.
2. Keep it. Re-read the mission statement once a year (the yearly cycle in the [weekly review](weekly-review.md)).
3. Move the decision-log entry into your own [decision log](../context-kit/decision-log.md) the same evening.

---

## 1. The mission statement

One sentence that describes the purpose of your future **portfolio**, not a single product.

- *Too narrow:* "To sell the best craft cocktail mixers in the city."
- *Better:* "To build a portfolio of small, well-run companies that make everyday rituals better, each one run by a founder and a staff of agents."

My mission statement: [one sentence]

Written on: [YYYY-MM-DD]

- [ ] It names a purpose that more than one company could serve.
- [ ] It is one sentence.
- [ ] I would still be glad to read it in five years.

## 2. The full-stack self-assessment

Rate yourself from 1 to 5 in each of the five areas. Be honest; nobody else sees this.

| Area | The question | My score (1 to 5) | One piece of evidence for that score |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Judgment** | Do you make decisions quickly and revisit them honestly? | | |
| **Taste** | Can you tell good work from bad in design, writing and code? | | |
| **Verification** | Do you check before you ship? | | |
| **Distribution** | Can you reach the people who will pay? | | |
| **Accountability** | Do you own outcomes, or explain them? | | |

**My lowest score:** [area]. This is where the book will ask the most of you. Note one thing you will do about it this month: [action].

## 3. Sketch your first machine as an organization chart

Pick one business idea, even a rough one: [idea, one line].

Under each function, write one job an agent could do and one decision that must stay with you.

| Function | What it holds | One job an agent could do | One decision that stays with me |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Research** | The agent instructions and sources you use to understand a market | [ ] | [ ] |
| **Product** | How you write specifications and decide what to build | [ ] | [ ] |
| **Engineering** | How agents build, test and ship your software | [ ] | [ ] |
| **Design** | Your brand and design system, and how new visuals are made from them | [ ] | [ ] |
| **Marketing, sales and support** | Your voice, your offers, your rules for when a human must step in | [ ] | [ ] |
| **Finance and legal** | How the books are kept, the numbers reviewed and the obligations met | [ ] | [ ] |
| **Operating rhythm** | The weekly routine in which you review the work, update the instructions and make the decisions only you can make | [ ] | [ ] |

Check the right-hand column against the five things Chapter 1 says never delegate:

- [ ] **Deciding what matters.**
- [ ] **Relationships** with customers, suppliers, partners and anyone who might invest in or buy the company.
- [ ] **Irreversible actions:** moving money, signing contracts, deleting data, making public statements, changing prices for existing customers.
- [ ] **Responsibility** for every consequence.
- [ ] **Your own mind:** a routine that protects your judgment, health and motivation (Chapter 15).

### Illustration

*Copper Pot Mixers is a fictional company used for illustration: a small Bengaluru business selling craft cocktail mixers to independent bars and cafes.* Three rows of its founder's sketch might read:

| Function | One job an agent could do | One decision that stays with the founder |
|---|---|---|
| Research | Find bars that opened in an area this quarter, with a link for each | Which neighborhood to sell into next |
| Marketing, sales and support | Answer delivery-day questions from the facts sheet | Every reply to a complaint about a damaged shipment |
| Finance and legal | Categorize the month's transactions and draft the summary | Every payment to a supplier |

## 4. Start the decision log

A single document where you write down each meaningful decision, its date and its reason. It is the first piece of your company's written memory, and written memory is what agents run on (Chapter 3). Use the [decision-log template](../context-kit/decision-log.md).

Your first entry can be tonight's:

```markdown
### D-1: [e.g. Our mission statement]
- **Date:** [YYYY-MM-DD]
- **Decision:** [the sentence you wrote above, or the idea you chose to explore first]
- **Reason:** [why, in two lines]
- **Revisit if:** [what would make you change it]
- **Status:** active
```

- [ ] Decision log created, with at least one entry.

## Where this breaks

- **Mistaking activity for progress.** A month of plans, pages and posts is not a customer. The next step after this page is research and ten conversations (Chapter 5), not a logo.
- **A flattering self-assessment.** A row of 4s and 5s with no evidence is a sign to score again.
- **A machine sketch with no right-hand column.** If every box says "agent", you have described a machine nobody is responsible for.
