# Pillar and spoke

*Companion to Chapter 11, "Marketing When Everyone Has AI", of* 1 Person, Billion Dollar Conglomerate *(2026 edition). Last reviewed: September 2026.*

## What this is

A content workflow built for leverage, not volume. **The pillar is yours:** one substantial piece of real substance (a story, an opinion, real numbers). **The spokes are the agent's:** adaptations of the pillar for each channel you use. **Every spoke gets a human edit** before it goes out. That edit pass is what separates a content machine from a slop machine, and it is not optional.

This file holds the workflow, a spoke brief, the edit-pass checklist, a voice-file template, the disclosure policy and the three measures to track.

## How to use it

1. Choose your two or three channels (where your customers actually are) and set a weekly volume cap. Write both at the top of your voice file.
2. Once a week or once a fortnight, produce one pillar. If you do not like writing, record yourself talking for twenty minutes and have an agent turn the transcript into a draft.
3. Brief the spoke agent. Run every spoke through the edit pass.
4. Every time you reject something, add it to the voice file with the reason.
5. Once a month, look at the three measures and cut channels that do not produce customers.

## The workflow

| Step | Who | What |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Pillar | You | One piece only you could write: a real customer story (with permission), a lesson from a mistake, your own numbers, an earned opinion |
| 2. Pillar edit | Agent, then you | Agent tightens and checks facts against the facts sheet; you approve |
| 3. Spokes | Agent | One adaptation per channel you use, within the volume cap |
| 4. Edit pass | You | Every spoke, every time, against the checklist below |
| 5. Schedule | Agent | Publishes the approved spokes (rung 2 for public statements: you approve each) |
| 6. Record | You | Rejections and their reasons go into the voice file |

**Fewer channels, done well.** Be absent from the rest without guilt.

**A volume cap.** Decide how much you will publish per week, and do not exceed it because an agent makes it easy. Quality compounds; volume decays.

## Spoke brief

> **Goal:** Adapt this week's pillar (attached) into [N] pieces for [channels], so that each channel's readers get the one idea in the form that suits them, and some of them move onto our email list.
>
> **Context:** Read the pillar, the voice file (including every rejected example and why), the facts sheet, the design system's imagery section, and the disclosure policy. Channel notes: [e.g. newsletter: 300 words, one story, one link; short post: under 60 words, one real photo; longer article: 800 words].
>
> **Constraints:** Only facts from the pillar and the facts sheet. No new claims, no statistics that are not in the pillar. No generated images of people or of customers; product images must be real photographs from [folder]. No health claims. Stay within the cap. Do not publish anything.
>
> **Done:** One draft per channel in [folder], each with a one-line note on the choice you made and the photograph you suggest.
>
> **Verification:** List every fact in each draft with its source line in the pillar or facts sheet. Check each draft against the rejected examples in the voice file and say which one it most risks resembling.
>
> **Questions:** Ask me if the pillar does not suit one of the channels (say so rather than force it), or if a draft would need a fact that is not in the sources.

## The edit-pass checklist

For every spoke, before it goes out:

- [ ] **It is ours.** It contains something only we could say: a real detail, a real customer, a real number, our opinion.
- [ ] **It does not sound like everyone else.** I cut every generic line ("In today's fast-paced world", "elevate your", "game-changer").
- [ ] **Facts are right.** Every fact matches the pillar or the facts sheet.
- [ ] **The product is represented truthfully.** Images show the real product, or faithfully match it.
- [ ] **No invented people.** No generated customers, experts or testimonials.
- [ ] **Labeled where required.** AI-generated content marked where the law requires, and per our disclosure policy.
- [ ] **Rights checked.** Any generated asset came from a tool whose terms allow commercial use.
- [ ] **One clear action**, ideally toward a channel we own (the email list, a tasting, a reply).
- [ ] **I would sign it.** I would be happy for a customer to know exactly how it was made.

This is the brand and voice guide from your context kit, extended for marketing. See also [voice-guide.md](../context-kit/voice-guide.md).

```
# Voice file: [Company]
Last updated: [YYYY-MM-DD]

## Who we are talking to
[The customer, in their words, and where they will read us.]

## How we sound
[Three to five principles, each with a one-line example.]

## Words we use / words we never use
Use: [ ]
Never: [ ]

## Channels and cap
[Channels, format for each, weekly cap.]

## Pieces we loved (and why)
1. [Paste the piece] Why: [specific reason]
2. ...

## Pieces we rejected (and why)
1. [Paste the piece] Why: [specific reason, e.g. "opens with a generic trend,
   not a customer"; "says 'healthy', which we cannot support"]
2. ...
(Add one every time you reject something in the edit pass.)
```

## Disclosure policy

Write a short policy, publish it on your website, and put it in the context kit so every marketing agent follows it. The version from Chapter 11:

> We use AI tools to help us write, design and run this company. Everything we publish is reviewed by a person who stands behind it. Photographs of our products are real. We never use generated images of customers or experts, and every testimonial we share comes from a real customer, with their permission. When you talk to an AI assistant on our website, it will tell you so, and you can always reach a person.

Adapt it to your company and check it against the rules where you operate. It is not legal advice; see the [legal starter](legal-starter.md).

## The three measures (monthly)

Views, likes and impressions measure activity, not business. Track these instead:

| Month | Where paying customers came from (count by source) | Cost per paying customer, by channel (including your time) | Owned audience (list size, change, how many bought) |
|---|---|---|---|
| [YYYY-MM] | [e.g. tastings 3, referrals 2, newsletter 1] | [e.g. tastings: 6 hours each; social: 10 hours, 0 customers] | [e.g. 240 subscribers, +18, 5 ordered] |
| | | | |

Ask every new customer how they heard of you, during onboarding. An agent tallies the answers.

## Worked example

*Copper Pot Mixers is a fictional company used for illustration.*

**Channels:** monthly newsletter to bars and cafes; one professional social network; the website's recipes page. **Cap:** three pieces a week.

**Pillar (the founder's own, recorded as a 20-minute voice note, turned into a draft, then rewritten):** "The kit nobody opened." For three months, tasting kits sat unopened behind bars. When she visited, a manager said: "My staff don't know what to do with the bottle." So she started adding a one-page recipe card and spending ten minutes with the staff. Reorders from bars that got the card and the ten minutes were noticeably higher than from those that did not (she shares her own small numbers, and says they are small).

**Spokes:**
1. Newsletter: the story in 300 words, with a photograph of a real bartender using the card (with permission), and an offer of a ten-minute staff session.
2. Social post: one sentence from the manager (with permission), a real photo of the card on a speed rail, one line: "Every kit now comes with this."
3. Recipes page: the three drinks from the card, with exact quantities from the facts sheet.

**Rejected in the edit pass, and added to the voice file:** a draft that opened "Bartenders everywhere are embracing craft mixers" (generic, unsupported) and a caption calling the pineapple mixer "a healthier choice" (a health claim the facts sheet does not support).
