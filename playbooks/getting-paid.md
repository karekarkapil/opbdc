# Getting paid

*Companion to Chapter 10, "The Launch Nobody Noticed", of* 1 Person, Billion Dollar Conglomerate *(2026 edition). Last reviewed: September 2026.*

> This is not financial, tax or legal advice. Payment rules, sales taxes and consumer rights differ by country and change. Set up payments and tax registrations with a qualified accountant, and check your terms with a lawyer, where you operate.

## What this is

A business is not a business until a stranger voluntarily pays for what you have made. This file gets you to that moment quickly and simply: the setup checklist for **hosted checkout or a payment link**, **your customers' payment methods**, **paid pilots**, **money actions on the trust ladder** (with the one goodwill-credit exception), and a cheap preparation for customers' buying agents. It is week 8 of the ninety-day plan in Chapter 21.

## How to use it

1. Work through the setup checklist once, before your first customer is offered the product. Charge from the first day.
2. Write every payment fact (methods, when payment is due, what counts as overdue) in your [facts sheet](../context-kit/facts-sheet.md) first; your terms and your agents take them from there.
3. Put each money action in the table below onto your [trust ladder](trust-ladder.md) and into your [permission matrix](../stack/permission-matrix.md).
4. Take your first payment, even a small one, this week.

## 1. Do not build billing

For your first customers, use the simplest hosted option your payment provider offers.

- [ ] **A payment link,** a **hosted checkout page**, or, for business customers, **an invoice with a payment link.** Pick one. Weeks spent on payment code are weeks not spent with customers.
- [ ] The payment provider handles card details. You never store card numbers, and no agent ever sees them.
- [ ] Payouts go to the company's own bank account ([finance stack](finance-stack.md)).
- [ ] If you sell across borders, ask your accountant whether a provider that acts as the **merchant of record**, handling sales taxes in each country for you, is right for you. Tax compliance is not where a one-person company should spend its early weeks.
- [ ] The payment provider is connected to your accounting software, so sales and fees flow into the books.

## 2. Use the payment methods your customers already use

In some markets that means cards; in others, instant bank transfers, wallets or payment on delivery. A beautifully designed checkout that does not offer the method your customer expects is a closed door.

- [ ] I asked my first customers how they pay their other suppliers, and I offer that.
- [ ] My facts sheet says, in one place: the methods, when payment is due, and what counts as overdue.

## 3. Charge for pilots

If a business customer wants to try before committing, offer a **paid pilot** at a modest price, with a clear end date and a clear definition of success. Free pilots teach you little and tend to last forever.

- [ ] Use the paid-pilot template in [first-ten.md](first-ten.md).
- [ ] The price, the end date and the success measures are agreed before the pilot starts, in writing.
- [ ] The founder sets and offers the price. Agents prepare pilot documents; they never offer or negotiate them.

## 4. Money actions stay at rung 2

Refunds, discounts, price changes and any credit above a small, fixed goodwill limit are **prepared by agents and approved by you**, as the trust ladder requires. Chapter 12 describes the one exception: a support agent may apply a small goodwill credit, up to a limit you set, to the customer's own account.

| Money action | Who prepares | Who approves or does it | Rung |
|---|---|---|---|
| Taking a payment through the hosted checkout or link | The customer | The payment provider | Not an agent job |
| Sending a routine invoice at your stated moment (dispatch or delivery) | Finance agent | Sent by the agent, within its rules | Up to 3 |
| One polite, pre-approved reminder when an invoice is overdue | Finance agent | Sent by the agent, within its rules | Up to 3 |
| Any firmer follow-up, and anything to a key account | Finance agent drafts | You send | 2 |
| Refunds | Support or finance agent prepares | You approve | 2 at most |
| Discounts and price changes | Agent may draft the notice | You decide | 0 to 2 |
| A goodwill credit up to your fixed limit, on the customer's own account, logged | Support agent applies it | Reviewed in your weekly sample | 3 at most: **the one exception** |
| Any credit above the limit, or outside its rule | Support agent prepares | You approve | 2 |
| Paying anyone (suppliers, refunds out, anything) | Nobody but you | You | Never delegated |

- [ ] The goodwill credit, if you allow one, is written in the facts sheet with its limit, its period and the mistakes it covers, and capped in the tool, not only in the instructions.
- [ ] Every credit is logged where you read it each week.
- [ ] No agent that reads customer messages holds any other money power (Chapter 14).

## 5. Selling to customers' agents: prepare cheaply

AI assistants that buy on a person's behalf are real, and early. Prepare cheaply; do not prioritize it.

- [ ] Prices, availability, delivery terms and the return policy are clear, current and written in plain text on the website, matching the facts sheet.
- [ ] A clean, accurate product list exists that could be exported as a feed.
- [ ] My payment provider is adding support for agent checkout, so switching it on later is a setting, not a project.

Then get back to your first ten human customers ([first-ten.md](first-ten.md)).

## Worked examples

*Copper Pot Mixers and Ledgerly are fictional companies used for illustration. Their facts sheets are the source of every value below.*

**Copper Pot Mixers** ([facts sheet](../context-kit/examples/beverage-company/facts-sheet.md)), selling to bars and cafes in Bengaluru:

- Methods: a payment link (card or UPI) or bank transfer, the methods bars already use. No credit terms: payment is due on delivery, and an invoice unpaid 48 hours after delivery is overdue.
- Invoices: sent by email at dispatch, with a payment link, by the finance agent (rung 3); one polite reminder once overdue; reminders to the top ten accounts are held for Meera (rung 2).
- Goodwill credit: the support agent may add up to Rs 300 per customer per month to the customer's own account, for a mistake that was the company's own, taken off the next invoice and logged (decision D-8). Damage in transit, refunds and replacements are Meera's.
- The four-outlet cafe chain was offered a tasting visit at one outlet, not a free month.

**Ledgerly** ([facts sheet](../context-kit/examples/software-company/facts-sheet.md)), selling software by subscription in the US and UK:

- Methods: card, through a hosted checkout, monthly; no invoices for plans. Sales taxes are added at checkout where they apply.
- Trial: 30 days, no card required. Refunds: the first month on request within 14 days; any other refund is the founder's.
- Failed payments: the hosted checkout emails a link to update the card, and Ledgerly sends one reminder ([finance brief example](../briefs/examples/finance.md)).

## Where this breaks

- **Free forever.** Pilots and trials with no price and no end. *Defense:* charge from day one; paid pilots with an end date.
- **Building billing.** Weeks lost to payment code. *Defense:* hosted checkout and payment links.
- **The wrong methods.** A checkout that does not offer what customers use. *Defense:* ask them.
- **Money powers creeping into agents.** A refund here, a discount there. *Defense:* rung 2 for every money action, with one capped, logged exception.
- **Chasing the agent economy too early.** *Defense:* prepare cheaply; serve humans first.
