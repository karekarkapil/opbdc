# Legal starter

*Companion to Chapter 14, "Law, Trust and Safety", of* 1 Person, Billion Dollar Conglomerate *(2026 edition). Last reviewed: September 2026.*

> **This is not legal advice.** Laws differ by country, by state and by industry, and the rules about AI are changing quickly. Use this file to know what to ask. Take it to a qualified lawyer where you operate, and do not rely on any template here until that lawyer has reviewed it.

## What this is

A checklist of questions to take to a lawyer, organized by the five areas of Chapter 14, plus the company itself. At the end, starting-point templates for terms of sale, a privacy policy, a refund and returns policy, and an AI disclosure notice.

## How to use it

1. Before you meet the lawyer, answer the "Your facts" lines in each section. An agent can draft them from your context kit; you check them.
2. Book one hour. Take the questions, your facts and the draft templates.
3. Write what the lawyer says into the [decision log](../context-kit/decision-log.md), dated.
4. Put the recurring items on your [compliance calendar](compliance-calendar.md).

## Context: the rules as of late 2026

As described in Chapter 14, and summarized here only as background for your conversation with a lawyer:

- **European Union:** new transparency duties began to apply in August 2026. People must be told when they are interacting with an AI system, and AI-generated content must be marked in a machine-readable way; generative tools already on the market were given until December 2026 to comply with the marking duty. Deepfakes and AI-generated text published on matters of public interest must be labeled, with an exemption where a person has genuine editorial control. Heavier obligations for "high-risk" uses (such as hiring, credit or essential services) were pushed back to December 2027 and August 2028.
- **United States:** unsettled. Several states require businesses to tell consumers when they are talking to an AI. The federal consumer-protection rule against fake reviews explicitly covers AI-generated testimonials.
- **Copyright:** courts continue to require human authorship for copyright protection.

Check each of these with your lawyer. They will have moved by the time you read this.

## 1. Honesty with customers

Your facts: [where customers talk to an AI agent: chat, email, voice], [where you publish AI-generated content], [where reviews or testimonials appear].

- [ ] Where I sell, what must I tell customers when they talk to an AI agent, and when (first message, every message, on request)?
- [ ] Which of my AI-generated content must be marked, and how?
- [ ] Is my published disclosure policy (Chapter 11) consistent with the law where my customers are?
- [ ] What are the rules on reviews and testimonials, including asking customers for them and quoting them?
- [ ] Are there rules on how product images must match what customers receive?

## 2. Data and privacy

Your facts: [what personal data you collect], [which agents and AI providers see it], [where it is stored], [countries your customers are in].

- [ ] Which data-protection laws apply to me, given where I and my customers are?
- [ ] What must my privacy policy say about AI providers that process customer information?
- [ ] Do I need a data processing agreement with each AI provider? What should it cover?
- [ ] Do my providers' terms allow them to train on my data? Is that acceptable for my customers' data?
- [ ] Which of my data counts as sensitive (health, financial, children's, government identity), and what extra protection does it need?
- [ ] What consent do I need so that customer data can pass to a new owner if I sell the company?
- [ ] What must I do if customer data is exposed?

## 3. Who owns what agents make

Your facts: [key creative assets: name, logo, product design, key content], [how a person contributed to each], [contractors who helped].

- [ ] Can my key creative assets be protected, given how they were made? What record of human contribution should I keep?
- [ ] Should I register my company name, product names and logo as trademarks, and where?
- [ ] What do my AI providers' terms say about ownership of output and any restrictions?
- [ ] Do I need written assignments from contractors or freelancers?
- [ ] Do the open-source licenses in my product carry obligations I must meet?

## 4. Contracts and terms

Your facts: [what you sell, to whom (consumers, businesses, both)], [payment terms], [delivery and return rules from the facts sheet].

- [ ] Please review my terms of sale, privacy policy and refund policy (templates below).
- [ ] Do I need a separate contract for business customers?
- [ ] How should I limit my liability, and what can I not exclude?
- [ ] Can my contracts be transferred if I sell the company?
- [ ] How should my supplier agreements handle bank-detail changes and fraud?
- [ ] When should I come back for another review? (Chapter 14: once, and again when the business changes.)

## 5. The rules of your industry

Your facts: [industry], [licenses you hold], [what is regulated about your product or service].

- [ ] Which licenses, registrations and permits does my business need, and how often do they renew?
- [ ] What labeling, safety or professional rules apply to my product or service?
- [ ] Does any use of AI in my business fall into a "high-risk" category where I sell?
- [ ] What records must I keep, and for how long?

## The company itself

Your facts: [country], [current structure], [plans for more companies].

- [ ] Which limited-liability form fits my company now?
- [ ] What insurance fits my risks: professional liability, cyber, product liability?
- [ ] If I plan a portfolio, what holding structure do you advise, and when should I set it up? (Chapter 17)
- [ ] What must be in the company's name rather than mine: domains, accounts, trademarks, contracts?

### AI disclosure notice

> **STARTING POINT ONLY: requires review by a qualified lawyer before use.**

For the first message of every customer-facing agent:

> Hi, I'm [Company name]'s AI assistant. I can help with [orders, delivery, product questions]. If you'd like a person at any point, say "person" and I'll pass you to [founder name or team], usually within [time].

For your website, next to the chat window and in your disclosure policy:

> We use AI tools to help us write, design and run [Company name]. Everything we publish is reviewed by a person who stands behind it. When you talk to our assistant, it is an AI, it will tell you so, and you can always reach a person at [contact].
