# Compliance calendar

*Companion to Chapter 14, "Law, Trust and Safety", and Chapter 17, "The Holding Company Machine", of* 1 Person, Billion Dollar Conglomerate *(2026 edition). Last reviewed: September 2026.*

## What this is

A starter calendar of the legal, financial and safety obligations a small company must not forget. Most are not complicated; they are simply easy to miss. **The agent prepares; you sign.** Signing remains a human act.

## How to use it

1. Ask your accountant and lawyer for the dates that apply to you, and fill in the "Due" column. **Tax and statutory dates depend entirely on your country, state and company form. Take them from your accountant, never from an agent's guess or from this file.**
2. Put every row into your calendar, with a reminder to the compliance agent [N] days before each due date.
3. Keep the evidence listed in the last column, in one folder per year. It is what a buyer, an auditor or a regulator will ask to see.
4. If you run more than one company, fill in the "Entity" column and keep one row per entity per obligation (see the portfolio section).

> This is not legal, tax or financial advice. Confirm every obligation and date with a qualified accountant and lawyer where you operate.

### On every change

Ask these whenever the business changes: a new country, a new customer type, new data, or a new agent with new powers.

- [ ] Does it change what we must tell customers about AI? (Honesty)
- [ ] Does it change what data we hold, who sees it, or where it goes? (Data and privacy)
- [ ] Does it create new assets we should own or protect? (Ownership)
- [ ] Do our terms, policies or contracts still describe what we do? (Contracts)
- [ ] Does it bring new industry rules, licenses or permits? (Your industry)
- [ ] Record the answer in the [decision log](../context-kit/decision-log.md), with the date.

## Brief for the compliance agent

> **Goal:** Make sure no obligation on the compliance calendar is missed, by preparing each one ahead of its due date so the founder only has to decide and sign.
>
> **Context:** Read this calendar, the compliance evidence folder, the facts sheet, the decision log, and the dates supplied by the accountant. Do not invent dates.
>
> **Constraints:** Prepare and remind only. Never file, pay, sign, accept terms or submit anything. Never contact a government body, lawyer or accountant without the founder's approval. For a portfolio, work on each entity with that entity's permissions only.
>
> **Done:** Every week, a short list: obligations due in the next [30] days, what is prepared, what is waiting for the founder, and anything overdue. Each prepared item is in the evidence folder, ready to sign.
>
> **Verification:** For each item, show the source of the due date (the accountant's note, the lawyer's note, or the official notice) and the document prepared. Mark any date with no source as "unconfirmed".
>
> **Questions:** Ask when a due date has no source, when an obligation seems new or changed, when the business has changed in a way listed under "On every change", or when anything asks you to submit or pay.
