# Weekly operating review

*Companion to Chapter 15, "The Operating Rhythm", and Chapter 17, "The Holding Company Machine", of* 1 Person, Billion Dollar Conglomerate *(2026 edition). Last reviewed: September 2026.*

## What this is

The heart of the operating rhythm: one sitting, once a week, at the same time, for about an hour. An agent prepares everything in advance, so the hour is spent deciding, not gathering. This file holds the agenda, the pre-read the agent prepares, the brief for that agent, the longer monthly, quarterly and yearly cycles, and a portfolio variant.

## How to use it

1. Put the review in your calendar: same day, same time, every week. [Monday, 9:00 to 10:00]
2. Schedule the preparing agent to run the evening before, using the brief below.
3. Work through the seven items in order, with a timer. Decide; do not gather.
4. Write decisions in the [decision log](../context-kit/decision-log.md) before you close the laptop.

Skipped for a month, this is how a company run by agents quietly goes wrong. Done calmly, whatever the numbers say, it is what lets you let go between reviews.

## The agenda (about an hour)

| # | Item | Time | What you do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The dashboard** | 5 min | Read the one-screen [CEO dashboard](ceo-dashboard.md). Note anything amber or red. |
| 2 | **Flags and escalations** | 10 min | Everything agents escalated this week that is still open, and everything unusual in the logs. Decide each item. |
| 3 | **The sample** | 20 to 25 min | Read a random selection of agent work: twenty support conversations, a few pieces of content, the code changes that went out, the finance agent's categorizations. This is how you catch drift. |
| 4 | **Context updates** | 10 min | Fix what the week revealed: a missing fact in the facts sheet, a new rule for an agent, a lesson for the lessons log, a decision for the decision log. |
| 5 | **The trust ladder** | 5 min | Promote a job that has earned more autonomy. Demote one that has had an incident. Note why. |
| 6 | **The one decision** | 10 min | Name the single most important decision only you can make: a price, a hire, a product direction, a customer relationship, something to stop. Make it, or schedule the conversation that will let you make it. Write it in the decision log. |
| 7 | **Next week's briefs** | 5 min | Queue the main work for the coming week, with briefs. |

## The pre-read (the agent prepares this)

```markdown
# Weekly review pre-read: week of [YYYY-MM-DD], [Company name]
Prepared by: [agent], [date and time].

## 1. Dashboard
[The one-screen dashboard. Amber and red items listed first, with the
threshold each crossed.]

## 2. Open escalations and flags
| # | From which agent | Date | What it is | Agent's suggestion | Your decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Unusual items from the logs this week:
- [item, with link to the log line]

## 3. The sample (chosen at random, not by the agent's preference)
- Support conversations (20): [links]
- Content published (3 to 5): [links]
- Code changes released: [links]
- Finance categorizations (10): [links]
Method of random selection: [describe]

## 4. Suggested context updates
| File | Suggested change | Evidence | Accept? |
|---|---|---|---|

## 5. Trust ladder candidates
| Job | Current rung | Suggest | Evidence (track record, incidents) | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## 6. Candidate for the one decision
[One or two candidates, each with the facts you need, the options, and
what happens if it waits another week. The agent does not recommend
an answer unless asked.]

## 7. Draft briefs for next week
[Each in six-part form: goal, context, constraints, done,
verification, questions.]
```

## Brief for the agent that prepares it

> **Goal:** Prepare the weekly operating review pre-read for [Company name] by [Sunday 5 pm], so the founder can spend the hour deciding, not gathering.
>
> **Context:** Read the dashboard data, every agent's escalation queue and logs for the past seven days, the trust ladder file, the facts sheet, the decision log, the lessons log, last week's pre-read and its decisions, and the backlog.
>
> **Constraints:** Read-only access to every system. Do not resolve escalations, change any agent's instructions, move any job on the trust ladder, or send anything to customers. Select the sample at random, and say how. Do not leave out anything unfavorable.
>
> **Done:** The pre-read in the template above, saved to [folder], with every item linked to its source.
>
> **Verification:** For each dashboard number, name the source and the time it was read. For each suggested context update, quote the evidence. List anything you could not reach or check.
>
> **Questions:** Ask before the review if a system was unreachable, if a number is missing, or if you find something that looks like an incident in progress. Anything urgent goes to the founder immediately, not into the pre-read.

## The longer cycles

### Monthly (about two hours)
- [ ] The finance close ([monthly close](monthly-close.md)).
- [ ] The numbers against the plan: revenue, margin, cash, new customers and where they came from.
- [ ] Cost per task and per customer for your main agents ([cost worksheet](../stack/cost-worksheet.md)).
- [ ] Review what your agents have remembered, and correct it.
- [ ] Count your customer conversations. If you spoke to fewer than a handful of customers yourself this month, that is the most important problem on the list.

### Quarterly (half a day)
- [ ] The [permission audit](permission-audit.md) and dependency licenses.
- [ ] The backup restore drill.
- [ ] The fifth-factor test: what breaks if your main provider doubles its price or disappears?
- [ ] The subscription review.
- [ ] A short strategy review: what to stop, what to start, what to continue. Written down.
- [ ] The [moat audit](moat-audit.md) (Chapter 18).

### Yearly (a day, away from the desk)
- [ ] Re-read your mission statement from Chapter 1, and decide whether it still holds.
- [ ] Look at the portfolio: which company to grow, which to fix, whether to start another.
- [ ] Look at yourself: peers, advisers, health, and whether the calendar still has customer time and deep time.

## Portfolio variant (Chapter 17)

With several companies, run one portfolio review instead of several full reviews. About seventy minutes for three companies.

| # | Item | Time | What you do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The portfolio dashboard** | 10 min | One line per company: cash and runway, revenue, margin, new customers, escalations waiting, agent spend, and a status of green, amber or red. |
| 2 | **Each company's exceptions** | 15 min each | Its flags, its escalations, its sample of agent work, its one decision. A green company may need five minutes; a red one gets the time it needs. |
| 3 | **Attention allocation** | 10 min | Which company gets more of your attention this week, and which can run on less? |
| 4 | **Cross-company lessons** | 5 min | Anything learned in one company that belongs in the shared skills library. Improvements travel as proposals: each company adopts them in its own review. |

For the portfolio, the preparing agent produces one pre-read per company, each prepared with that company's permissions only, plus a one-page portfolio summary. Monthly: each company's close and the combined position. Quarterly: each company's audits, and one hard question per company: grow it, keep it steady, fix it, or close it?
