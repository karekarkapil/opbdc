# Brand and voice guide (template)

*Companion to Chapters 3, 11 and 12 of* 1 Person, Billion Dollar Conglomerate *(2026 edition). Last reviewed: September 2026.*

## What this is

How the company sounds, in every email, reply, post and page. A few sample paragraphs are worth more than a page of adjectives: show a reply you love and a reply you hate, and say why. Every marketing, support and sales agent reads this file. How the company **looks** lives in [`design-system.md`](design-system.md).

## How to use it

- Write three to five principles, each with a short example.
- Collect real samples: replies and posts you approved, and ones you rejected, with the reason.
- Every time you reject an agent's draft, add the reason here. "Too corporate" teaches an agent nothing; "Three exclamation marks and no answer to the question" does.
- Keep your published disclosure policy here too, so every agent follows it.

See filled-in versions: [beverage company](examples/beverage-company/voice-guide.md), [software company](examples/software-company/voice-guide.md).

## Guidance notes

- **Examples beat adjectives.** "Friendly but professional" means different things to every reader. Two replies, one right and one wrong, mean one thing.
- **Customer-facing agents always say what they are.** The first thing a chat or voice agent says is that it is an AI assistant, and how to reach a person (Chapters 12 and 14).
- **Borrow attitude, not appearance.** Name brands whose behavior you admire and say what you admire; do not copy their words.

## Where this breaks

- **All adjectives, no examples.** Agents produce the average of your adjectives.
- **Never updated.** If the rejection log is empty, you are fixing drafts by hand and not teaching the agents.
- **One voice for every situation.** An apology for a broken bottle and a launch post need different registers. Show both.
