# Customer file (template)

*Companion to Chapters 3 and 5, "Context Is the Company" and "A Problem Worth Solving", of* 1 Person, Billion Dollar Conglomerate *(2026 edition). Last reviewed: September 2026.*

## What this is

Your customers, in their own words. Verbatim quotes from conversations, reviews, complaints, support tickets and visits, each with a date and a source. This is where the sentence on the napkin belongs.

Agents are good at generalizing. They need the raw material of real voices to generalize from. A summary is an opinion; a dated quote is evidence.

## How to use it

- Add to it **the day** you hear something revealing. Capture is cheap at the moment and expensive afterward.
- Copy words exactly, including grammar you would not use yourself. Do not tidy them.
- Record where each quote came from, so you or an agent can check it.
- Agents may add summaries of calls (see [`../playbooks/customer-conversations.md`](../playbooks/customer-conversations.md)), but the verbatim quotes are the core.
- Personal data: record the customer's type and a short reference, not their phone number or email. Contact details live in your customer system, not in files agents read.

See filled-in versions: [beverage company](examples/beverage-company/customer-file.md), [software company](examples/software-company/customer-file.md).

## Template

```markdown
# Customer file: [Company name]

Last checked: [YYYY-MM-DD]

## Who our customers are
[Two or three sentences in plain words. Link to the company brief for the full definition.]

## The sentences that changed our mind
Keep the few quotes that changed how you see the problem at the top.
- "[Verbatim quote]" ([customer type], [source], [YYYY-MM-DD]). What it taught us: [one line].

## Quotes by theme
Themes are added as they appear. An agent can propose themes; you confirm them.

### [Theme, e.g. "Ordering late at night"]
- "[Verbatim quote]" ([customer type], [source: call / visit / email / review / ticket], [YYYY-MM-DD])
- "[Verbatim quote]" ([customer type], [source], [YYYY-MM-DD])

### [Theme]
- "[Verbatim quote]" ([customer type], [source], [YYYY-MM-DD])

## What they have tried
- [Workaround, tool or person they pay], ([customer type], [YYYY-MM-DD]). Cost to them: [money, time].

## What they pay for today
- [Existing spend related to the problem, with source.]

## Conversation summaries
One entry per conversation. Newest first.

### [YYYY-MM-DD]: [customer type], [call / visit / message]
- Situation: [their business, size, role]
- Problem in their words: "[quote]"
- What they have tried: [ ]
- What it costs them: [ ]
- Surprises: [ ]
- Follow-up: [what you promised, and by when]
```

## Guidance notes

- **Past, not future.** "Last Friday we ran out" is a fact. "I would probably use that" is politeness. Record the facts.
- **Money and workarounds are the strongest signal.** Note every time a customer says what they already spend.
- **Tag the source.** Quotes from a paying customer, a lost customer and a stranger on a forum carry different weight.
- **Keep negative quotes.** The complaint you would rather forget is the one an agent most needs to see.

## Where this breaks

- **Summaries replacing quotes.** If the file is all paraphrase, agents generalize from your interpretation, not the customer's reality.
- **Undated quotes.** Customers change. A quote from two years ago may describe a problem you already solved.
- **Personal data creep.** Names, phone numbers and order details belong in your customer system, with its access controls.
