# Example brief: writing

*Companion to Chapters 3 and 11, "Context Is the Company" and "Marketing When Everyone Has AI", of* 1 Person, Billion Dollar Conglomerate *(2026 edition). Last reviewed: September 2026.*

*Copper Pot Mixers is a fictional company used for illustration.* The founder sends a short newsletter to bar and cafe owners once a month. After a bland draft, she added a rule to the voice guide: newsletters always open with a real customer.

## The brief

> **Goal:** Draft this month's newsletter for bar and cafe owners on our list. Its job is to get managers to try the Tender Coconut and Lemongrass mixer before the festival weekends, when they need a strong non-alcoholic option on the menu.
>
> **Context:** Read the voice guide (especially the newsletter section and the examples of posts I loved and rejected), the facts sheet (products, prices, delivery days, cut-off), and the customer file. Open with the story from my visit last Tuesday to a cafe in Indiranagar: the owner's line, recorded verbatim in the customer file, is the opening. My notes from that visit are pasted below this brief.
>
> **Constraints:** Under 300 words. One call to action only: reply to order a tasting kit, or reorder. No discount (we are not running one). No health claims. Do not invent any quote, number or customer; use only what is in the customer file and my notes. The owner agreed to be quoted by first name only.
>
> **Done:** A draft in the marketing folder, with a subject line and two alternatives, and a note on anything you left out and why.
>
> **Verification:** List every factual statement (price, date, product detail) with the facts-sheet line it came from. Confirm the opening quote matches the customer file word for word. Read it once as a tired bar owner at midnight on a phone, and tell me which sentence you would cut.
>
> **Questions:** Ask me before mentioning any product, event or offer that is not in the facts sheet, or if my notes and the customer file disagree about what the owner said.

## Why this brief works

- The **substance is the founder's**: a real visit, a real quote, used with permission. The agent shapes it; it does not invent it. That is the difference between a content machine and a slop machine.
- The **voice guide carries the style**, so the brief does not need adjectives like "friendly but professional".
- The **constraints close off the usual failures**: invented testimonials, made-up offers, creeping length.
- **Evidence** is a list of facts with sources, not "I checked everything".

## What to check when it comes back

- [ ] Read it aloud. Does it sound like you, or like everyone else's newsletter this month? Cut anything generic.
- [ ] Compare the opening quote with the customer file, word for word.
- [ ] Check every price and date against the facts sheet yourself (three at least).
- [ ] One call to action, not three.
- [ ] Nothing that the disclosure policy forbids: no generated customers, no generated testimonials.
- [ ] Record: if you rewrote a sentence, add the before and after to the voice guide's examples, with the reason.
