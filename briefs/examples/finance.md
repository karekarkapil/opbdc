# Example brief: finance

*Companion to Chapters 3 and 13, "Context Is the Company" and "The AI CFO", of* 1 Person, Billion Dollar Conglomerate *(2026 edition). Last reviewed: September 2026.*

*Ledgerly is a fictional company used for illustration.* Ledgerly bills every plan monthly by card through its hosted checkout; it sends no invoices. When a renewal payment fails (usually an expired or replaced card), the hosted checkout emails the customer a link to update the card, and the facts sheet promises one reminder from Ledgerly. A handful of this month's payments have failed. The founder wants the reminders sent politely, using wording approved in advance. The finance agent's standing permissions (read, categorize, match, draft; no authority to pay anyone) are in the [finance stack](../../playbooks/finance-stack.md).

This is not financial advice. Adapt collection practices to the law where you operate.

## The brief

> **Goal:** Follow up this month's failed card payments, so money owed to us comes in without damaging relationships with customers we want to keep.
>
> **Context:** Read the failed-payments report in the payment provider, the facts sheet ("Trials, billing and refunds": one reminder, data never deleted for a failed payment), the collections section of the finance playbook (it holds the approved routine reminder and the firmer follow-up the founder may send), and each customer's record in the customer file. The customer file marks our five most important accounts as "key".
>
> **Constraints:** Send at most **one** routine reminder per failed payment, using only the approved wording; you may fill in names, amounts, dates and the update-card link, and nothing else. Anything after that one reminder (a second message, the firmer follow-up) is drafted and held for me, never sent. Never threaten, never mention locking or deleting data, never add a fee, never offer a discount or a payment plan, never discuss a dispute. Do not pay, refund or credit anyone. Do not contact a key account at all: draft the message and hold it for me.
>
> **Done:** For each failed payment: the one routine reminder sent (non-key accounts that have not had one), or a draft held for me (key accounts, anyone already reminded, anything in dispute). A table: customer, plan, amount, date of the failed payment, days since, action taken or proposed, and any reply received.
>
> **Verification:** Before sending anything, check in the payment provider that the payment is still failed and the card has not been updated since, as of today, and show the check. Confirm that each message sent matches the approved template word for word apart from the filled fields. List any customer who replied with a complaint or a question.
>
> **Questions:** Ask me before contacting anyone who has written to us about a problem in the last 30 days, anyone whose failed amount does not match their plan, or anyone who cancelled in the same week.

## Why this brief works

- **Approved wording** means the agent's tone cannot drift. The relationship with a customer whose card expired in a hard month is worth more than one month's fee.
- **One routine reminder, then the founder.** The agent does the routine; anything firmer is a decision about a relationship, and stays with the founder.
- **Key accounts and disputes come to the founder.** The agent reminds; the founder handles the relationship.
- **Checking the provider before sending** prevents the most embarrassing error in collections: chasing a customer who has already paid.
- **No money moves.** Agents keep the books; you move the money.

## What to check when it comes back

- [ ] Pick two "sent" reminders and confirm, in the payment provider yourself, that those payments were still failed when the reminder went out.
- [ ] Read every held draft before it goes. Personalize the key accounts.
- [ ] Any reply that mentions a problem: answer it yourself.
- [ ] Update the money-owed line on your [CEO dashboard](../../playbooks/ceo-dashboard.md).
- [ ] Record: if you changed a template or made a new rule, write it into the finance playbook.
