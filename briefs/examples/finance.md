# Example brief: finance

*Companion to Chapters 3 and 13, "Context Is the Company" and "The AI CFO", of* 1 Person, Billion Dollar Conglomerate *(2026 edition). Last reviewed: September 2026.*

*Ledgerly is a fictional company used for illustration.* Ledgerly bills some of its Bookkeeper-plan customers by monthly invoice. A handful are overdue. The founder wants them chased politely, using wording approved in advance. The finance agent's standing permissions (read, categorize, match, draft; no authority to pay anyone) are in the [finance stack](../../playbooks/finance-stack.md).

This is not financial advice. Adapt collection practices to the law where you operate.

## The brief

> **Goal:** Chase this month's overdue invoices, so money owed to us comes in without damaging relationships with customers we want to keep.
>
> **Context:** Read the accounts-receivable report in the accounting software, the collections section of the finance playbook (it holds the three approved reminder messages: gentle, second, final), and each customer's record in the customer file. The customer file marks our five most important accounts as "key".
>
> **Constraints:** Use only the approved reminder wording; you may fill in names, amounts, dates and the payment link, and nothing else. Never threaten, never add a late fee or interest, never offer a discount or a payment plan, never discuss a dispute. Do not pay, refund or credit anyone. Do not contact a key account at all: draft the message and hold it for me.
>
> **Done:** For each overdue invoice: the reminder sent (for non-key accounts up to 30 days overdue) or a draft held for me (key accounts, anything over 30 days, anything in dispute). A table: customer, invoice number, amount, days overdue, action taken or proposed, and any reply received.
>
> **Verification:** Before sending anything, check each invoice is still unpaid in the accounting software and the bank feed, as of today, and show the check. Confirm that each message sent matches an approved template word for word apart from the filled fields. List any customer who replied with a complaint or a question.
>
> **Questions:** Ask me before contacting anyone who has written to us about a problem in the last 30 days, anyone whose invoice amount does not match their plan, or anyone whose payment might be in transit.

## Why this brief works

- **Approved wording** means the agent's tone cannot drift. The relationship with a customer who is late because the month was hard is worth more than the invoice.
- **Key accounts and disputes come to the founder.** The agent reminds; the founder handles the relationship.
- **Checking the bank before sending** prevents the most embarrassing error in collections: chasing a customer who has already paid.
- **No money moves.** Agents keep the books; you move the money.

## What to check when it comes back

- [ ] Pick two "sent" reminders and confirm, in the bank feed yourself, that those invoices were unpaid when the reminder went out.
- [ ] Read every held draft before it goes. Personalize the key accounts.
- [ ] Any reply that mentions a problem: answer it yourself.
- [ ] Update the money-owed line on your [CEO dashboard](../../playbooks/ceo-dashboard.md).
- [ ] Record: if you changed a template or made a new rule, write it into the finance playbook.
